## Study A — neutral, diagnosed before it is optimized

**Question.** Four explanations compete for weak neutral performance: class imbalance, ambiguous
annotation, weak representation, and a misplaced decision boundary. Each calls for a different fix.

### What was measured

| | Validation (checkpoint) | Train, out-of-fold (5 fold models) |
|---|---:|---:|
| Macro-F1 | 0.8634 | 0.8420 |
| Neutral precision / recall / F1 | 0.804 / **0.562** / 0.661 | 0.696 / **0.535** / 0.605 |
| Neutral errors predicted with p ≥ 0.9 | **66%** | **72%** |
| Mean p(gold) on neutral errors | 0.090 | 0.073 |
| Neutral examples every fold model gets wrong | 33% of dev neutral | — |

The fold models select checkpoints on validation exactly as the main runs do, and each train example
is scored only by the model that never saw it.

### What it says about the four explanations

1. **Decision boundary: weak evidence.** A bias on the neutral log-probability, tuned on the OOF train
   predictions and applied to validation, settles at **+0.10**, a small shift. It moves validation
   macro-F1 from 0.8634 to 0.8672 and neutral F1 from 0.661 to 0.672, well inside the ±0.027 interval
   a single dev run carries. This agrees with ADR-015, where cross-fitted threshold tuning was worth
   zero. Moving the boundary does not recover the missing neutral recall.
2. **Why the boundary cannot help: the errors are confident.** Two thirds of the neutral errors are
   predicted at p ≥ 0.9 for another class. On train, **33% of all gold-neutral examples** are
   confidently assigned elsewhere by a model that never saw them. No threshold reaches those
   without also misclassifying large numbers of positive and negative sentences.
3. **Annotation vs representation: not separable from predictions alone.** A confident model that
   disagrees with gold looks the same whether gold is wrong, ambiguous, or right and the model is
   wrong. Confident Learning makes the same point. Separating them is the human audit's job
   (below).

### Descriptive profile (train OOF, gold neutral, n = 458)

| Contains | n | Error rate | vs without |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| contrast connective (*nhưng, tuy, …*) | 24 | **0.92** | 0.44 |
| intensifier (*rất, quá, lắm, …*) | 29 | **0.83** | 0.44 |
| hedge (*tạm, bình thường, ổn, …*) | 45 | 0.62 | 0.45 |
| politeness formula (*cảm ơn, ạ, …*) | 40 | 0.23 | 0.49 |
| fewer than 5 syllables | 88 | 0.22 | 0.52 |

The neutral sentences the model gets right are short and formulaic. The ones it gets wrong carry
evaluative language. **These are co-occurrences, not causes**, and the supports are small. A reading of
a sample of the confident errors did *not* show one dominant pattern. It showed a mix of implicit
suggestions without a *nên / cần / mong* cue, evaluative sentences where a neutral gold looks
questionable, fragments that need context, and plain model errors. How those groups divide is exactly
what the audit must establish, so no share is claimed here.

### What was produced for the audit

- `audit_sample_index.csv`: **160 examples** (80 train, 80 validation), stratified by confusion cell
  (most confident errors first), plus the least certain correct predictions per class and **20
  uniformly random examples per split** as a control.
- `local/audit_sheet.csv` (gitignored): the sheet itself, with model columns placed after the annotator
  columns. Annotation rules are in [ANNOTATION_GUIDE.md](../../docs/ANNOTATION_GUIDE.md).
- `train_predictions.csv`: OOF probabilities, uncertainty and fold id for all 11,426 train rows, the
  input for ranking suspected label issues.

**Status: the audit itself has not been done.** It is a human step, and this README will not
substitute a model's opinion for it.

## Calibration (E09)

Temperature fitted on one stratified half, scored on the other, then swapped
([protocol](../../docs/EVALUATION_PROTOCOL.md#8-calibration-and-selective-prediction)). 15 bins.

| View | n | Fitted T (two halves) | NLL | Brier | ECE equal-width | ECE equal-mass |
|---|---:|---|---|---|---|---|
| Checkpoint on validation | 1,583 | 1.61 / 1.50 | 0.206 → **0.170** | 0.080 → 0.077 | 0.034 → **0.014** | 0.034 → 0.014 |
| Fold models on train (OOF) | 11,426 | 1.54 / 1.52 | 0.212 → **0.177** | 0.089 → 0.084 | 0.035 → **0.014** | 0.035 → 0.018 |

- **The model is overconfident, and consistently so.** The mean confidence is 0.986 against an
  accuracy of 0.953. Four independent temperature fits agree on T ≈ 1.5–1.6, so the fit is a stable
  property of this recipe, not noise.
- Temperature scaling preserves every prediction (asserted in code), so it changes no F1. Its value
  is probabilities that mean what they say: NLL drops 17%.

### Abstention hides neutral

| Coverage kept (most confident first) | 100% | 95% | 90% | 80% |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| Error rate on kept examples | 4.7% | 2.9% | 2.3% | 2.0% |
| **Share of neutral kept** | 100% | **69%** | **48%** | **37%** |
| Share of negative / positive kept | 100% | 96% / 97% | 90% / 94% | 70% / 92% |

Rejecting the least confident 10% halves the error rate. It does so by discarding more than half of
the neutral class. An abstention rule reported only by its aggregate risk would look like a clean
win. It is not one.

## Robustness (Study B, suite version 1)

Perturbations are applied to raw text, then segmented with pyvi, as the service would do. Differences
are paired on the same 1,583 validation examples, with 95% bootstrap intervals over examples.

| Suite | Changed | Macro-F1 | Δ [95% CI] | Share predicted neutral |
|---|---:|---:|---|---:|
| clean | — | 0.8634 | — | 3.2% |
| `nodiacritic` (all marks stripped) | 99.7% | **0.268** | **−0.595** [−0.634, −0.555] | **64.1%** |
| `nodiacritic-50` (half the syllables) | 97.2% | 0.652 | −0.211 [−0.252, −0.175] | 11.9% |
| `teencode-30` | 19.3% | 0.848 | −0.015 [−0.029, −0.005] | 3.5% |
| `teencode-100` | 49.4% | 0.821 | −0.042 [−0.067, −0.017] | 4.5% |
| `charnoise-5` | 79.1% | 0.784 | −0.079 [−0.111, −0.050] | 3.7% |
| `charnoise-10` | 92.3% | 0.769 | −0.094 [−0.133, −0.058] | 5.1% |

- **Without diacritics the pipeline stops working**: 0.268 is barely above the majority-class floor
  (0.225). It also fails in a telling way. Predictions collapse into neutral, from 3% to 64%, so
  neutral *recall* rises from 0.56 to 0.80 while everything else is lost. A per-class metric read on
  its own would call that an improvement.
- The corpus is 99.86% diacritized (ADR-007), so this is invisible in the benchmark and matters only
  in deployment, where unaccented typing is common. It measures the whole pipeline: pyvi also
  segments unaccented text poorly, and this suite does not separate the two failures.
- Teencode costs little per changed sentence, and most sentences contain no dictionary word to replace.

### Negation minimal pairs ([data/probes](../../data/probes/README.md))

| Set | Pairs | Pair accuracy | Flip rate |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| positive → negated (*không nhiệt tình*) | 36 | **1.00** | 1.00 |
| negative → negated (*không khó tính*), expected labels arguable | 8 | 0.25 | 0.63 |

Negating a positive predicate always works. Negating a negative one mostly does not yield positive:
3 pairs stay negative and 3 become neutral. This fits the corpus, where 84% of sentences containing a
negation cue are gold-negative (EVALUATION_PROTOCOL § 6). The model appears to have learned
*không* as a negative-sentiment cue more than as a polarity operator. With 8 pairs and arguable
labels, that is a hypothesis for a larger probe, not a finding.

### Slices of clean validation (slice version 1)

| Slice | n | Accuracy | Macro-F1 | Macro-F1 usable? |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---|
| negation | 294 | 0.956 | 0.869 | yes |
| short (< 5 syllables) | 76 | 0.895 | 0.888 | yes |
| contrast | 57 | 0.930 | 0.826 | no: 2 neutral examples |
| suggestion | 247 | 0.980 | 0.619 | no: 1 neutral example |
| long (≥ 30 syllables) | 100 | 0.970 | 0.818 | no: 3 neutral examples |

A slice's macro-F1 is only reported as usable when every class has at least 5 examples. The
`suggestion` slice has 247 sentences and one neutral, so its 0.619 says nothing.

## Cycle 1 — declared interventions (`cycle1/`)

`vifeedback study cycle1` applies the decision rules committed in `configs/experiments/cycle1.yaml`
before any run. Output: `cycle1/decisions.json`. Per-run metrics and validation predictions are in
`results/runs/p8-*`. The laptop runs were regenerated after `results/` was lost (ADR-023); training is
deterministic and the regenerated numbers are checked against the original logs.

| Hypothesis | Declared prediction | Result (validation, seed-paired vs same-seed control) | Decision by the declared rule |
|---|---|---|---|
| **H1a** logit adjustment τ=1 | < 0.01 macro-F1; neutral recall ↑, precision ↓ | +0.0029 mean [−0.047, +0.053], 2/3 seeds; neutral P 0.79→0.61–0.70, R 0.56→0.66–0.67 | **Not advanced** (needs ≥ +0.005). Prediction confirmed |
| **H1b** balanced head retraining (cRT) | same | +0.0028 mean [−0.025, +0.031], 2/3 seeds; neutral P → 0.59–0.69, R → 0.64–0.77 | **Not advanced.** Seed spread 0.0012 vs 0.011 for CE (observation) |
| **H2** diacritic/teencode augmentation, 30% | ≥ 20% less `nodiacritic-50` degradation, ≤ 0.005 clean loss | **5 seeds** (finalist): degradation 0.217 → 0.124 (**−43%**); full no-diacritic 0.28 → 0.65; teencode −54%; clean mean +0.004, but per seed −0.006 … +0.030 (median −0.002); character noise unchanged (−4%) | **Supported at 5 seeds.** Targeted, in-family robustness at no material clean cost |
| **H3** XLM-R on raw text | raw − pyvi > 0 | Kaggle, same session, 5 seeds: **+0.0097** [+0.0014, +0.0179], 4/5 seeds, p = 0.032. Raw XLM-R 0.8499 vs PhoBERT-base 0.8643 | **Supported**: segmented input cost XLM-R ~0.01. Raw mean below the 0.8523 withdrawal threshold, so ADR-016's narrowed conclusion stands: ~40% of the gap was preprocessing, ~60% remains |
| **H4** shared encoder, λ=0.3 | helps / harms / no difference | sentiment +0.0012, topic +0.0005 (3 seeds) | **No material difference**: one model serves both tasks at half the inference cost, no loss detected |
| **H4** shared encoder, λ=1 | same | sentiment −0.0056 (0/3 seeds), topic +0.0031 (2/3) | **Negative transfer on sentiment** by the rule (interval still spans 0) |


**What Cycle 1 establishes.**

1. **The neutral gap is not in the classifier.** Two independent ways of re-placing the decision
   boundary (training-time logit adjustment, post-hoc balanced head retraining) and Study A's post-hoc
   bias all move macro-F1 by noise-sized amounts. Each one trades neutral precision for recall. The
   remaining explanations, label ambiguity and representation, need the human audit.
2. **Robustness to missing diacritics is cheaply trainable, but only in-family.** Augmentation repairs
   the perturbations it was trained on at no material clean cost, and leaves character noise it never
   saw unchanged (at 3 seeds it looked slightly harmful; at 5 it is −4%, i.e. nothing). It is a
   targeted fix, not general robustness.
3. **XLM-R's deficit was partly a preprocessing artifact.** Fed raw text it gains +0.010 over pyvi
   input in the same session, but still trails PhoBERT-base by 0.014. Its robustness is not better
   where it matters: without diacritics 0.35 (PhoBERT 0.27, PhoBERT + augmentation 0.65), with half
   the diacritics 0.66 (PhoBERT 0.65). Augmentation buys more robustness than the architecture swap.
4. **Sharing the encoder costs nothing at λ = 0.3 and something at λ = 1.** The engineering reading is
   one model instead of two for serving. The scientific reading is that the tasks' measured
   dependence (Cramér's V 0.344) does not translate into a gain at this data size.
