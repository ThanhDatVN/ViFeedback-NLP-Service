# Annotation Guide — Neutral-Label Audit (Study A)

**Purpose.** Decide *why* the model is weak on `neutral` before trying to fix it. The four candidate
explanations — class imbalance, ambiguous annotation, weak representation, a biased decision
boundary — call for different interventions ([REVIEW_AND_RESEARCH_PLAN.md § 7, Study A](REVIEW_AND_RESEARCH_PLAN.md)).
This audit measures the second one. It does not relabel the corpus.

**Scope.** Sentiment task only; `train` and `validation` only. **The test split is never audited,
relabelled or used to choose between annotation policies.** Changing test labels to make the model
look better is ruled out in advance (Study A, output clause).

**Materials.** `vifeedback study neutral-audit` writes the sheet to
`results/studies/study_a/local/audit_sheet.csv`. That folder is gitignored because it contains corpus
text ([DATA_CARD.md § 11](DATA_CARD.md)); the committed file `audit_sample_index.csv` records which
rows were sampled, so the sheet can be regenerated byte-for-byte from the same seed.

---

## 1. Procedure

1. **Blind pass.** Open a copy of the sheet and hide `stratum`, `gold`, `gold_assessment` and every
   column to the right of `notes`. `stratum` names the error cell (`error:neutral->negative` reveals
   both the gold label and the prediction); the columns after `notes` hold the model's prediction,
   its confidence and the linguistic flags. Fill `annotator_label`, `neutral_subtype` and `notes`
   from the sentence alone.
2. **Gold comparison.** Only then compare with `gold` and fill `gold_assessment` (§ 4).
3. **Model columns last.** Reveal the model columns only after steps 1 and 2 are done for the whole
   sheet. They are there for later analysis, not to help the annotator decide.
4. Do not return to change a blind-pass label after seeing the gold or model columns. If you
   realise you misread a sentence, add a note and keep the original label.

Annotate the sentence as written. Do not guess the course, the lecturer or the rest of the survey
form. If the sentence cannot be judged without that context, that is itself the answer (§ 3,
`insufficient_context`).

### Corpus conventions

| Token | Meaning | How to treat it |
|---|---|---|
| `wzjwz` + digits | An anonymised person or course name | A name. Carries no sentiment. |
| `colonsmile`, `colonlove`, `colonbigsmile`, … | An emoticon rewritten as a word | Evidence of the writer's tone. Weak on its own. |
| `colonsad`, `coloncontemn` | A negative emoticon | Same: tone, weak on its own. |
| `doubledot`, `dotdotdot` | Punctuation (`:`, `...`) rewritten as a word | No sentiment. |

Text is lowercase. Missing diacritics and teencode are part of the data, not errors to fix.

## 2. Sentiment labels

The label describes the **attitude the student expresses toward the subject** of the sentence
(teaching, curriculum, facilities, the lecturer).

| Label | The sentence… | Constructed examples (not from the corpus) |
|---|---|---|
| `positive` | expresses approval, satisfaction or praise | *thầy giảng dễ hiểu* · *phòng học mát, sạch sẽ* |
| `negative` | expresses disapproval, dissatisfaction, a complaint or a problem | *slide chữ quá nhỏ, khó nhìn* · *cô hay đến muộn* |
| `neutral` | expresses **no evaluative attitude**, or one too balanced or too unclear to call | *môn học có 3 tín chỉ* · *em không có ý kiến* |
| `ambiguous` | *annotator-only.* Two careful readers could reasonably disagree, and neither reading is a misreading | — |

`ambiguous` exists so that uncertainty is recorded as uncertainty, instead of being forced into a
label and later mistaken for disagreement with gold.

**Suggestions are the hard case, and the corpus has already decided them.** *thầy nên cho thêm bài
tập* ("the teacher should give more exercises") reads naturally as a neutral request. The dataset
does not treat it that way: **91.1% of sentences containing *nên* / *cần* / *mong* are gold
`negative`** (measured, [ADR-007](DECISIONS.md)). The original annotators read a request for change
as implied criticism of the current state.

This audit measures errors *against the dataset's own policy*, not against a new one, so this guide
follows that convention:

- a suggestion or request for change → `negative`, subtype `request_suggestion`
  (*nên giảng chậm lại* · *mong khoa mở thêm lớp buổi tối*);
- a request that implies no deficiency — a question, a request for information → `neutral`,
  subtype `request_suggestion` (*cho em hỏi lịch thi khi nào*);
- if you personally read a suggestion as neutral, label it `ambiguous` and say so in `notes`. Do not
  label it `neutral`: that would record a disagreement with the policy as a disagreement with gold.

The subtype is recorded either way, so the size of this group can be counted.

---

## 3. Neutral subtypes

Fill `neutral_subtype` whenever `annotator_label` is `neutral` or `ambiguous`, **or** `gold` is
`neutral`. Otherwise write `n/a`. Choose the first one that applies:

| Subtype | Definition | Constructed example |
|---|---|---|
| `objective_fact` | A factual statement with no evaluation | *lớp học vào buổi sáng thứ hai* |
| `no_opinion` | The student says they have nothing to add, or gives a non-answer | *không có ý kiến gì* · *bình thường* on its own |
| `request_suggestion` | A request, question or suggestion. Recorded whatever the label; § 2 sets the label | *cho em hỏi lịch thi khi nào* |
| `mixed` | Both positive and negative evaluations, with neither dominating | *thầy nhiệt tình nhưng giảng hơi nhanh* |
| `insufficient_context` | Evaluative words are present, but what they refer to, or which way they point, needs context the sentence lacks | *cũng được* · *như vậy là ổn rồi ạ* |
| `other` | None of the above. **Describe it in `notes`.** If several `other` items share a pattern, it is a new subtype | — |

`mixed` is the one to watch. UIT-VSFC assigns one sentiment per sentence, so a sentence praising the
lecturer and criticising the room has no correct single label. The review flags exactly this
(§ 3, aspect-level sentiment). A high `mixed` share among neutral errors is evidence for the
aspect-level extension, not for a better classifier.

---

## 4. Assessing the gold label

`gold_assessment` has three values. Choose the most conservative one that applies.

| Value | Use when |
|---|---|
| `agree` | Your blind label matches gold, **or** differs from it but gold is a defensible reading |
| `ambiguous` | The sentence genuinely supports more than one label, and gold is one of them |
| `incorrect` | Under any reasonable reading, following this guide, gold is not the right label |

`incorrect` is a strong claim and the default is *not* to make it. A model that confidently disagrees
with gold is evidence for inspection, not proof of a labelling error: a confident wrong model and a
wrong gold label look identical from the model's side (Northcutt et al., 2021,
[Confident Learning](https://arxiv.org/abs/1911.00068)). Write the reason in `notes` for every
`incorrect`.

`ambiguous` versus `incorrect` is the distinction the study exists to measure. An `ambiguous`
example says the task definition is under-specified. An `incorrect` one says the annotation process
made a mistake. They lead to different interventions (label smoothing or a revised taxonomy for the
first, label correction for the second).

## 5. Agreement and adjudication

- **Double annotation.** A second annotator labels at least 50 sheet rows, including every row in
  the `random` stratum, blind to the first annotator.
- **If there is no second annotator.** Re-annotate the same 50 rows blind after at least 24 hours,
  from a fresh copy of the sheet. Report the result as **intra-annotator** agreement and say so: it
  measures one person's consistency, not whether the labels are shared, and is a weaker figure.
- **Agreement.** Report Cohen's κ on `annotator_label` overall and on `neutral`-vs-rest, with the
  number of items. Compare against the agreement reported in the dataset paper
  (Nguyen et al., 2018), not against 1.0: disagreement is expected on this task.
- **Adjudication.** Disagreements are discussed and resolved into `adjudicated_label` (added at
  adjudication time). If the two annotators still disagree after discussion, the adjudicated label
  is `ambiguous`. Both original labels are kept; nothing is overwritten.
- **Guide changes.** If adjudication shows a rule in this guide is unclear, fix the guide, bump its
  version below and note which rows were labelled under which version. Do not silently re-annotate.

---

## 6. Reporting rules

1. **Report rates within their stratum.** The sheet is a *targeted* sample: error cells and
   low-confidence rows are over-represented by design. "40% of the neutral→positive errors audited
   are `mixed`" is a valid statement. "40% of the corpus is mixed" is not.
2. **Only the `random` stratum estimates a corpus-level rate**, and it is small (20 rows per split
   by default). Report it with a Wilson interval, which stays honest at small n.
3. **Say what was not found.** If the audit shows gold is mostly `agree` on neutral errors, the
   annotation-noise explanation is weakened. That is a result, and it points the next cycle at
   representation or the decision boundary.
4. **Corrected labels are an intervention, run separately.** If enough `incorrect` labels are
   confirmed, training on corrected labels is experiment E04, compared against original labels
   under an otherwise identical configuration. Evaluation labels stay unchanged either way.

---

## 7. What this audit cannot establish

- **Causation.** It shows which error types exist. It does not show that fixing one would raise
  macro-F1; only the controlled interventions in Study A steps 4 and 5 can.
- **Annotator intent.** It measures whether gold is *defensible under this guide*. The original
  annotation guidelines are not part of the distributed data used here. This guide reconstructs the
  one policy that can be measured from the labels (suggestions, § 2); where it diverges from the original on anything else, a
  disagreement is a policy difference, not an error.
- **Anything about test.** It is out of scope by construction.
