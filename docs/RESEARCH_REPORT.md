# Reliable Vietnamese Feedback Classification under Class Imbalance and Input Shift

**Technical report · Cycles 0–2 · 2026-09-27.** UIT-VSFC sentiment (3 classes) and topic (4 classes).
Every number here is regenerated by a CLI call from files in the repository. Cycle 1 is closed:
all four hypotheses were decided by pre-declared rules, and the finalists were evaluated on test once.
Cycle 2 moved confirmation to new data (a frozen challenge set). H5 and H6 are decided, and H7 has its
pilot. The declared LLM run and the API arm are pending (§ 5.8).

---

## 1. Problem and scope

Student feedback in Vietnamese, classified by sentiment and topic. The benchmark hides two problems.
Neutral is **4.3%** of sentiment labels and carries a third of macro-F1. And real users type without
diacritics or in teencode, which the 99.86%-diacritized benchmark never shows. The project asks what
limits the minority class, how reliable the model's confidence is, and what survives realistic input,
under a laptop-GPU budget (RTX 3050, 4.29 GB) plus free Kaggle.

**Not in scope** (per the external review): new product features, dashboards, online deployment.

## 3. Method

- **Data.** Official splits 11,426 / 1,583 / 3,166, pinned to a Hub commit and verified against a
  committed content hash. 55 test rows overlap train after normalization; a slice without them is
  frozen for the closing gate.
- **Metric.** Macro-F1 as the headline, with per-class precision/recall/F1. A classifier that never
  predicts neutral scores 0.947 accuracy and 0.649 macro-F1 on the test split.
- **Seeds and pairing.** Five fixed seeds for results, three for exploration. Comparisons pair runs by
  seed; cross-architecture pairs are labelled as sharing data order only.
- **Test discipline.** Test was touched only at Gate G4 (21 logged rows). No decision since has used a
  test number. Everything in Cycles 0–1 is on validation or out-of-fold train predictions.
- **Pre-registration.** Cycle 1's hypotheses, controls, metrics, decision rules and 30-run budget were
  committed before its first run (`configs/experiments/cycle1.yaml`, commit 0f3055d; H4 added as
  v2 in 060902d, also before its runs). The rules are code (`evaluation/decisions.py`), so the
  decision cannot drift toward the numbers.
- **Determinism.** Ten same-seed re-runs across phases, and all Cycle 1 CE controls, reproduce earlier
  runs **exactly**. Seed variance is the only run-to-run variance. fp16 evaluation can flip a tied
  example: one neutral sentence moves dev macro-F1 by 0.004, which bounds how small a single-seed
  difference can be and still mean something.

## 4. Baseline results (test, 5 seeds)

| Pipeline | Macro-F1 | Neutral F1 |
|---|---|---|
| PhoBERT-base + VnCoreNLP (best measured) | 0.8373 ± 0.0031 | 0.595 |
| PhoBERT-base + pyvi (deployed) | 0.8288 ± 0.0108 | 0.571 |
| TF-IDF word+char + dev-fitted priors | 0.7450 | 0.421 |
| Topic: PhoBERT-base + pyvi | 0.8038 ± 0.0045 | others 0.553 |

Word segmentation is worth +0.0234 macro-F1 (5/5 seeds, p = 0.001), replicating the conditional
finding of arXiv:2301.00418 for BPE models. A published figure (BamiBERT, 83.41) is context, not a
ranking: its averaging and seed protocol are not documented in comparable detail.

## 5. Findings

### 5.1 Neutral errors are confident, and the boundary is already right (Q1)

On validation and out-of-fold over train, neutral recall is 0.56 / 0.53 while precision is
0.80 / 0.70. **66–72% of neutral errors are predicted at p ≥ 0.9**, and 33% of gold-neutral training
examples are confidently assigned elsewhere by a model that never saw them. A neutral bias tuned on
out-of-fold predictions moves dev macro-F1 by +0.004, which is noise.

**H1 tested the classifier directly.** Both logit adjustment (τ = 1) and balanced head retraining (cRT)
leave macro-F1 unchanged: +0.003 mean each, 2/3 seeds, 95% intervals spanning ±0.03–0.05. Neither
meets the declared advance rule. cRT was later run at two more seeds as H2's controls; at 5 seeds its
mean is +0.0006. Both trade neutral **precision** (0.79 → 0.59–0.70) for **recall**
(0.56 → 0.64–0.77). cRT also shrinks seed spread (0.8640 ± 0.0012 vs 0.8612 ± 0.011, 3 seeds): an
observation, not a declared hypothesis.

**Conclusion so far:** the neutral gap is not a threshold or head problem. What remains is whether the
confident errors are label policy/ambiguity or representation, and only the human audit can separate
those (160-row stratified sheet and annotation guide ready). A reading of a sample shows a mix of
implicit suggestions without a cue word, evaluative sentences labelled neutral, context-free fragments
and plain model errors, so no single cause is claimed.

### 5.2 Diacritics break the pipeline; augmentation repairs it in-family (Q2)

Stripping all diacritics drops validation macro-F1 from 0.863 to **0.268**, with predictions
collapsing into neutral (3% → 64%), which makes neutral *recall* rise. Half-stripping gives 0.652;
30% teencode costs 0.015.

**H2, augmentation at 30% exposure (diacritic removal, teencode), is supported at 5 seeds:** the
half-stripped degradation falls 43% (threshold 20%), full stripping recovers from 0.28 to 0.65, and
teencode's degradation falls 54%. Clean accuracy is flat: the mean is +0.004, but one seed contributes
+0.030 and the typical seed moves −0.002. **It does not generalize out of family:** on character
noise, never used in training, it changes nothing (−4% relative; the −20% seen at 3 seeds was noise).

Negation is learned one-directionally: 36/36 positive→negated minimal pairs flip correctly, 2/8
negative→negated pairs reach positive. The negative-pair labels are arguable, so this is a hypothesis
for a larger probe.

### 5.3 XLM-R's deficit was partly preprocessing, mostly model (R8, H3)

In one Kaggle session, XLM-R-base on raw text beats XLM-R-base on pyvi-segmented text by **+0.0097**
[+0.0014, +0.0179], 4/5 seeds, p = 0.032. The tokenizer profile explains why: on segmented input XLM-R
produces 36% more subwords. But raw XLM-R reaches 0.8499, still 0.014 below PhoBERT-base (0.8643) and
below the pre-declared withdrawal threshold of 0.8523. So ADR-016's narrowed conclusion stands, now
quantified: about 40% of the original gap was a preprocessing artifact, 60% is the model.

The same session re-ran PhoBERT-large and XLM-R from Tier E. **Every row matched the original,
recorded weeks apart, to four decimals**, and same-session repeats were identical: seed-controlled
training is reproducible on Kaggle as well as on the laptop.

Robustness of the alternative encoder (Study B): raw XLM-R without diacritics scores 0.35 (PhoBERT
0.27, PhoBERT + augmentation 0.65). With half the diacritics removed it is no better (0.66 vs 0.65).
A different architecture buys less robustness than augmentation does.

### 5.4 Sharing the encoder costs nothing at λ = 0.3, and sentiment at λ = 1 (Q4)

One PhoBERT encoder with a sentiment head and a topic head, `L = L_sent + λ·L_topic`, epoch chosen by
the mean of the two dev macro-F1 scores, against single-task runs at the same seeds:

| λ | Sentiment Δ | Topic Δ | Declared verdict |
|---|---|---|---|
| 0.3 | +0.0012 (2/3 seeds) | +0.0005 (1/3) | no material difference |
| 1 | **−0.0056 (0/3)** | +0.0031 (2/3) | negative transfer on sentiment |

At λ = 0.3 one model serves both tasks at half the inference cost with no loss detected at 3 seeds.
That is not an equivalence claim. At λ = 1 the topic loss pulls the shared representation away from
sentiment. The measured dependence between the tasks (Cramér's V 0.344) does not become a gain at this
data size.

### 5.5 Confidence is overconfident but ranks errors well (Q5)

Four independent temperature fits agree on T ≈ 1.5–1.6. Scaling cuts NLL by 17% and ECE from 0.034
to 0.014 and changes no prediction. Max-probability, margin and entropy rank errors equally well
(AURC 0.012 against 0.047 random, 0.001 oracle). **Abstention hides the minority class:** keeping the
most confident 90% halves the error rate but keeps only 48% of neutral examples.

### 5.6 What the serving artifact preserves (Q6)

The export now goes through a staged release (ADR-020): calibration and acceptance through the
checkpoint's own preprocessing, the full validation set, FP32 logit parity (measured 8.2e-5), INT8
non-inferiority at a 0.005 macro-F1 margin, and a checksummed manifest. Measuring the old silent
fallback, a pyvi-trained model fed raw text, gave **−0.052 macro-F1** and neutral F1 0.672 → 0.557,
more than twice the documented figure. The service now reports itself not ready instead.

**INT8 does not pass the gate** (ADR-022). Dynamic INT8 shrinks the model 4× (540 → 136 MB), leaves
both majority classes unchanged, and drops **neutral F1 from 0.672 to 0.584**: macro-F1 −0.030,
one-sided upper bound 0.054 against a 0.005 margin. Label agreement is 98.7%, so an accuracy-style
check would have shipped it. Static per-tensor INT8 breaks the model (0.342). The first Kaggle attempt
failed earlier still: quantizing a fused, `ENABLE_ALL`-optimized graph defeats shape inference. The
pipeline now quantizes the pre-processed plain export, and ships FP32 optimized only to the portable
EXTENDED level.

**Latency on the reference CPU** (AVX2, no VNNI; two steady passes agreeing within 1%):

| Configuration | p50 | p95 | vs L0, p50 / p95 | texts/s (b=32) | Quality |
|---|---:|---:|---|---:|---|
| L0 PyTorch FP32, pad to 96 | 117.4 ms | 120.5 ms | 1× | 12.0 | reference |
| L1 PyTorch FP32, dynamic padding | 46.7 ms | 64.1 ms | 2.5× / 1.9× | 24.1 | identical |
| **L3 ONNX FP32 (served)** | **15.5 ms** | **33.6 ms** | **7.6× / 3.6×** | 24.9 | identical (parity 8.2e-5) |
| L4 ONNX INT8 dynamic (blocked) | 9.0 ms | 20.4 ms | 13.1× / 5.9× | 40.9 | neutral F1 −0.088: not released |

The served FP32 ONNX artifact is 7.6× faster than padded PyTorch at the median with identical
predictions. The project's original H3, that INT8 might be *slower* without VNNI, is falsified: it is
1.7× faster than FP32 ONNX. Speed was never INT8's problem here; the minority class is. Absolute
latency on a laptop shifts with its power state between sessions. Three later sessions on AC power
(served augmented model, same graph) measured L3 p95 at 19.2 and 20.0 ms where a steady pass
existed, and L0 at 70–75 ms instead of 117 ms. So the 30 ms p95 target is met, the table above is a
slow-state session, and the p50 speed-up over padded PyTorch is 6.3–7.6× depending on the state.

### 5.7 Closing gate: the finalists on test, once

The deployed CE model and the H2-augmented model were evaluated on test once, with everything frozen
beforehand (ADR-024). Over 5 seeds the augmented model's test macro-F1 is unchanged (+0.0008
[−0.012, +0.013]). Its seed-42 checkpoint scores 0.635 without diacritics on test, against 0.271 for
CE. A temperature fitted only on validation cuts test NLL by 21% and ECE from 0.042 to 0.015 for both.
Excluding the 55 test rows that overlap train moves macro-F1 by at most 0.0007.

### 5.8 Cycle 2: new evaluation data, a serving decision, and an LLM reference (Q2, Q5, Q7, Q8)

The official test had been evaluated 28 times, so Cycle 2 confirms on data it has never touched: a
**challenge set** of 305 constructed sentences in 10 categories, checked against the corpus and frozen
by SHA-256 before any model saw it (ADR-026). Its typed noise was written by hand, not by the
augmentation code, so it is the first natural-noise test of H2.

**H6: the service now runs the augmented model** (ADR-027). On hand-typed noisy rows its accuracy is
0.800 against 0.611 for CE (+0.189 [+0.067, +0.311]); on all other rows both score 0.867. The
declared rule is met. Pooling hid two regressions, which the decision records: hand-typed teencode
0.975 → 0.875 (scripted teencode had improved), and short factual sentences 0.767 → 0.633, drifting
to `negative` at confidence 0.5–0.7. Unaccented typing, the case that motivated H2, rises from
0.32 to 0.74. This is the project's first decision influenced by a test result, and is logged as one.

**H5: sparse and dense topic models are not complementary enough to stack.** An out-of-fold
logistic meta-model over PhoBERT probabilities and TF-IDF scores loses to the PhoBERT fold ensemble
alone (−0.0102 [−0.0228, +0.0013]) and lowers `facility` and `others`. The motivating observation,
TF-IDF ahead on `facility` (0.921 vs 0.905, one model each), reverses under a paired interval:
−0.021 [−0.060, +0.015]. It was a single-model difference inside noise.

**H7 pilot: a 1.7B instruction model is far behind, and disagrees with the corpus's label policy.**
Qwen3-1.7B with a prompt frozen on a train subset (label likelihood, thinking disabled) reaches
validation macro-F1 0.68 zero-shot and 0.65 with six demonstrations, against 0.86 for the encoder;
neutral F1 0.26–0.29 against 0.66. The failure is neutral **precision** (0.16–0.18): the model calls
requests for change and contrast sentences neutral, which the corpus labels polar even when the
prompt states the convention. Yet on factual sentences it scores 0.97–1.00 on the challenge set, where
the encoder scores 0.63–0.77. The encoder and the LLM fail on different neutral cases: the encoder
misses non-evaluative text, and the LLM rejects the annotation policy. This is the same split the human
audit is designed to measure (Q1). The pilot is not the declared result. Qwen3-4B (Kaggle) and
gpt-4o-mini (API, challenge set only until the dataset licence is confirmed) are pending.

**Out-of-scope input (Q5).** On 20 sentences that are not course feedback, both encoders give a
polar or neutral label with mean confidence 0.86–0.90, and 65–70% of them above 0.9. Max-probability
does not flag off-topic input; an abstention rule would need an out-of-scope detector.

## 7. Limitations

- **One benchmark, one domain.** No natural external sample. The challenge set adds hand-written
  input variation and out-of-scope sentences, but one person wrote and labelled all 305 rows, so it
  tests phenomena, not the distribution of real feedback, and has no agreement estimate.
- **Small minority support.** 73 neutral examples on validation; 3-seed intervals are wide. The
  decisions say "not resolved at this budget", never "equal".
- **Calibration and selection share the validation set.** Cross-fitting separates fitting from scoring
  the temperature, but not from checkpoint selection; the out-of-fold view is fully independent.
- **Per-run artifacts from Phases 2–4 were lost** in an accidental deletion; gate aggregates survived.
  Example-level paired comparisons are possible only for runs from Cycle 1 on.
- **Label questions are unresolved** until the human audit; no test label is ever changed.

integration, API contract and image-build jobs.
